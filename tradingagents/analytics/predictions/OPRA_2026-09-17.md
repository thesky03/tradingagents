# Opera Limited (OPRA) — the discount, decomposed

Frozen 17 Sep 2026. Every figure below comes from secondary reporting of
Opera's filings; this environment blocked sec.gov, investor.opera.com and
every finance domain, so **no primary filing was read**. Confidence flags
reflect that ceiling.

## The premise needs two corrections before the analysis starts

**1. Only one of the named assets is actually hidden.** Of OPay, MiniPay,
Opera News, Opera Neon and Opera GX, only **OPay** sits outside the
consolidated P&L. The rest are *inside* reported revenue or contribute
approximately nothing to it — Q2 2026 advertising ($115.0M) plus query
($62.1M) is $177.1M of $178.1M total revenue. **There is no third
revenue line.** MiniPay, Neon and GX are segmental disclosure gaps, not
off-balance-sheet value, and adding them to a sum-of-the-parts would
double-count.

**2. The stakes portfolio is nearly empty.** Nanobank sold 2022,
StarMaker sold 2022, Fjord Bank exited 2024. "Opera holds several
stakes" is a historical thesis, not a current one.

## OPay: the one number, and what is inside it

**9.5% at 31 Dec 2025, carried at $294.6M**, implying $3.10B for the
whole company. That figure is **Opera's own model**: 18.5% cost of
equity, probability-weighted present values from $0.2B to $4.7B,
weighted average $3.4B, 10% marketability discount — and **an 85%
probability of an OPay IPO within two years** baked in.

The last actual priced round was **2021, $400M Series C at $2B**. There
has been no third-party mark in five years.

The underlying business did inflect, on audited numbers:
revenue **$536.25M (+161%)**, net profit **$72.47M** from a $50.98M
loss, GTV **$358.0B (+115%)**, MAU 39.3M. At $3.1B that is 5.8x revenue
and 43x earnings. Risks: **88.1% of revenue is Nigeria**, and loan
originations grew 285% to $938M.

**And OPay's chairman is Yahui Zhou — Opera's own Executive Chairman.**
Opera's largest non-core asset is a stake in a company controlled by
Opera's own chairman.

**Most of the gap is the standing cost of the structure.** A minority
slice of a controlled company with a 32% float and no index eligibility
trades at a discount whether or not anyone is behaving badly.

## Why Kunlun caps the multiple — four mechanisms, in order of size

**1. Arithmetic, not intent.** 68% held → 32% float → Cayman-domiciled
foreign private issuer fails the US-domicile test for the Russell
indexes → institutions own ~17% → the marginal buyer is retail. Nothing
about this is a decision anyone makes each morning; it is the structure.

**2. Disclosure.** Foreign private issuer status means **20-F annually,
no 10-Q, no proxy statement**. Quarterly results are voluntary 6-K press
releases, not reviewed filings. Audited financials arrive once a year.
That alone thins the analyst base.

**3. A related-party record that makes governance-sensitive capital
pass.** Individually defensible, collectively a pattern:
- **Star X sold to Kunlun** (Apr 2022) for $83.5M — Opera claimed a 178%
  return and $26.4M above carrying value, but on **multi-year vendor
  financing at 3.5% simple interest**, which in 2022–23 was below
  Opera's own opportunity cost. Opera financed its parent's purchase of
  an asset from itself.
- **The dividend set-off.** Of the Jan 2023 special dividend of $71.3M,
  **$59.0M payable to Kunlun and Keeneyes was offset against receivables
  owed by them** rather than paid in cash, and Opera disclosed that
  future dividends on Kunlun's shares would continue to be offset until
  the Star X receivable was settled. Minorities received cash; the
  parent received a set-off.
- **Marketable securities sold to Kunlun ($51.2M) and Keeneyes ($7.8M)**
  in Jan 2023, priced on a 15-day average, settled the same way.
- **The Nanobank receivable converted into OPay shares at $35.9M below
  book value.**
- No independent fairness opinion on any of it was found. Opera is a
  Nasdaq "controlled company" and is **exempt from the majority-
  independent-board requirement**.

**4. The buyback as Kunlun's funding channel — the finding that matters
most.** Kunlun Tech's FY2025: revenue RMB 8.198B (+44.8%), **net loss
RMB −1.593B**, **operating cash flow RMB −736M (down 354%)**, cash of
roughly RMB 1.1B (~$150M). **Opera is ~53% of Kunlun's revenue and its
only profit centre**; the domestic AI business loses money.

Opera's $300M buyback is structured **pro rata** — open-market ADSs plus
ordinary shares bought from Kunlun — so Kunlun's stake stays at 68%. At
68%, roughly **$204M of that $300M flows to Kunlun**.

So "Kunlun keeps 68%" and "Kunlun is steadily monetising" are **both
true at once**, and reporting only the unchanged percentage misses the
cash flow entirely.

## Where the "suppression" framing is wrong

**The pro-rata buyback is defensible, and arguably minority-protective.**
Buying only public ADSs would shrink the float in both absolute and
percentage terms and push Kunlun toward 72%+. Pro rata holds the float
at 32% and retires shares for everyone — and Kunlun is selling its own
stock at $15.21 to do it, which is accretive to whoever stays. What the
structure does *not* do is let the float **grow**, so the route to index
eligibility stays closed by design.

**HFCAA risk is largely mechanically inapplicable.** Opera's auditor is
**KPMG AS, Oslo** — its auditor since 2000. HFCAA delisting risk keys off
**auditor jurisdiction**, not shareholder nationality, and a Norwegian
firm is fully PCAOB-inspectable. The PCAOB's China determinations were
vacated in Dec 2022 in any case. No source naming Opera on the SEC's
Commission-Identified Issuers list was found.

The *real* China risk is different, and Opera flags it itself: Zhou is a
PRC national subject to PRC law, so PRC action could affect Kunlun's
ability to exercise control. The concrete precedent is that **CFIUS
forced Kunlun to sell Grindr** — a completed US action against this
exact shareholder.

**Hindenburg is largely spent.** The Jan 2020 report targeted $2.60 on
70% downside. The lending apps were quietly brought into compliance and
then divested entirely — Nanobank sold Mar 2022 for $127.1M. The
securities class action (*Brown v. Opera*, SDNY) was **dismissed on the
pleadings in Mar 2021** for failure to plead misrepresentation and
scienter, then dismissed with prejudice as to the named plaintiffs under
Rule 41(a)(2) — plaintiffs walked rather than re-plead. No merits
adjudication either way; the related-party allegations were never tested.

## The catalyst, and whether the gap can close

A discount that nothing forces to close is not a return. Here something
might:

**The commitments are not funded.** $300M buyback + ~$143M of two-year
dividends = **$443M** against $145M cash plus roughly $253M of two-year
free cash flow = **$398M**. A $45M shortfall. Either the buyback
under-executes, FCF grows faster than assumed, or **an asset is sold —
and the only saleable asset is OPay.**

Opera's own accounting already assumes an **85% probability of an OPay
IPO within two years**, and in December 2025 OPay installed a
listing-ready management team including **former Opera CEO Lars Boilesen
as co-CEO** and an ex-Citigroup MD as CFO.

## What would falsify this

- **If OPay does not file to list within two years**, Opera's own 85%
  assumption was wrong and the $294.6M carrying value is overstated.
- **If the buyback completes at $300M without an asset sale**, the FCF
  base was stronger than the FY2025 figure implied.
- **If the float ever widens**, the single largest component of the
  discount can compress; under the current buyback design it cannot.
- **If Nigeria deteriorates** — 88% of OPay revenue, with loan
  originations up 285% — the stake mark falls regardless of Opera.

## The operating risk nobody is discussing

MAU went **284M → 288M** while ARPU went **$1.97 → $2.46**. Essentially
**all revenue growth is price, not users.** That is the same shape as a
monetisation-per-unit story with a ceiling, and it is the thing to watch
rather than the governance.

## Sample bias worth naming

Every accessible valuation source says Opera is cheap — Simply Wall St
(~25% undervalued), GuruFocus (16.7%), Alpha Spread (45%), seven of
seven analysts rating Buy with an average $26.29 target. **No published
bear case on valuation from 2025–26 was found.** Read that as a
one-sided sample, not as corroboration. And no source anywhere has
actually isolated a control discount from a China discount from a
liquidity discount — anyone quoting "the Opera China discount is X%" is
quoting something nobody has computed.
