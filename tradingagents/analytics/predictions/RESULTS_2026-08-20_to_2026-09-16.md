# Out-of-sample result: 2026-08-20 → 2026-09-16 (27 days)

Tested against `PREDICTIONS_2026-08-20.csv` (sha256 8a62aa22…), frozen
before any September data was observed.

## Result: negative. SKY ranked these 17 names backwards.

| Cohort | n | Mean 27-day return |
|---|---|---|
| GENERATIONAL (80+) | 3 | **−12.5%** |
| CORE (70–79) | 9 | −1.3% |
| ACCUMULATE and below | 5 | **+0.8%** |
| S&P 500 | — | −1.59% |

Information coefficient, Spearman:

- All 17 names: **−0.236 ± 0.25** — inside one standard error of zero,
  so formally "no signal", but signed the wrong way.
- The 9 names whose August baseline price was high-confidence:
  **−0.600 ± 0.35** — roughly 1.7 standard errors from zero.
- Structural-only variant: −0.270 / −0.467. Stripping the price-derived
  fields did not rescue it.

The tier ordering is monotonically inverted. That is worse than noise
would usually look, and it is recorded here rather than explained away.

## What this test can and cannot establish

It CANNOT establish that SKY is broken. n=17 over 27 days, with a
standard error of 0.25, is not a verdict on a score whose stated
horizon is a decade and which deliberately excludes momentum. The
window also contained a regime shock: the 10-year yield reached its
highest level since 2007, WTI rose ~28% on Middle East supply
disruption, the Nasdaq 100 shed 5% in an AI-led selloff, and the
market traded into the first expected Fed hike since 2023.

It CAN establish that the score offered no protection in that shock,
and that the three names it was most confident about were the three
that fell hardest. Confidence and outcome moved in opposite directions,
which is the specific failure mode a high-conviction tier is supposed
to avoid.

## Two blind spots this exposed

1. **No discount-rate channel.** Measured directly: moving the 10-year
   from 4.70% to 5.00% — the actual move, to a 19-year high — changes
   SKY's cross-sectional ranking by a Spearman correlation of 1.0000
   and a mean of 0.047 points per name. The loop-5 regime test flagged
   this as theoretical in August. It is no longer theoretical.

2. **No input-cost or commodity channel.** WTI +28% is a first-order
   cross-sectional event. BKNG, the #2 ranked name, is a travel
   business and fell 18.3%. Nothing in the score connects an energy
   shock to a consumer-discretionary cost structure.

## What was deliberately NOT done

The weights were not refitted to this window. Refitting a ten-lens
model on 17 observations of 27-day noise would manufacture a flattering
backtest and destroy the score's out-of-sample meaning — the precise
error this predictions directory exists to prevent.

A duration/rate channel is worth adding on first principles (the
present value of distant cash flows falls when the discount rate
rises; this is arithmetic, not a fitted pattern). Note honestly that
it would NOT have fixed this window: the long-duration names here
(NVDA at ~29x, +26.3%) outperformed the short-duration ones (ADBE at
~10x, −6.3%), because an earnings surprise swamped the rate effect.

## The real test

A decade-horizon score needs multi-year holding periods and a universe
wide enough to diversify idiosyncratic earnings events. Until then,
every IC in this directory is a sanity check. This one did not pass.
